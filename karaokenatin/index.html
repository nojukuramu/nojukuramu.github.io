KaraokeNatin

One screen sings. Every phone is a remote.
Peer-to-peer over WebRTC — no account, no install, no server of ours.

Host

Open this on the TV, laptop, or whatever screen everyone can see.

Join

Scan the host's QR code, or type the six-character room code.

How does this work without a server?

Phones and the host screen talk directly to each other over a WebRTC data channel. The only thing two browsers cannot do alone is find each other in the first place, so the app borrows a public PeerJS broker as a meeting point: it passes along a few lines of connection details keyed by your room code, and then it is out of the loop. Your queue, your names, and your commands never touch it.

Song search goes through public Piped mirrors, falling back to Invidious if those are down. If every mirror is unreachable you can still paste a YouTube link — that path needs nobody's API.

Practical limits: the host tab has to stay open, and a very restrictive network (some corporate or campus Wi-Fi) can block peer-to-peer entirely.
